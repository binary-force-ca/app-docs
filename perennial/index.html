Perennial - Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 18, 2026

Perennial: Habit Tracker helps you keep daily and weekly habits. Your habits stay on your device. The app collects nothing about you and sends nothing to us. It has no account and no server, and it works offline. The only third party involved is Google, which serves the one small banner ad at the bottom of the screen. If you allow it, that ad can match your interests; if you don't, or where the law asks first, it doesn't. The app works the same either way.

Information the app stores

Perennial keeps its data only on your own device:

  • your habits: each habit's name, emoji, type (daily or weekly), scheduled weekdays, the day you created it, the days you completed or skipped it, and when it was archived or restored
  • if the stored habits are ever found damaged, a copy of that damaged data, kept on the device so nothing is lost
  • on iPhone, one yes-or-no note: whether the app has already asked Apple's tracking question, so it never asks on its own twice. Your answer itself is kept by iOS, not by the app.
  • There is no account, no sign-up and no login.
  • There is no Perennial server. Nothing you enter is uploaded to us.
  • We cannot see your habits. Nobody can retrieve them from us, because we never receive them.
  • The app works fully offline. Only the banner ad needs a connection.

Because this data lives only on your device, deleting the app deletes it, and you can delete any single habit inside the app. If your device backs itself up (to iCloud on an iPhone, or with Android's backup to your Google account), Perennial's data may be included in that backup, which is governed by Apple's or Google's privacy policy, not this one.

Information the app does not collect

The app does not collect, transmit or have access to:

  • your name, email address, phone number or postal address
  • your precise location. The app never asks for location access. (Google may estimate a coarse location from your IP address to serve an ad; see Advertising below.)
  • your contacts, photos, camera, microphone or health data
  • usage analytics or crash reports of our own. The app contains no analytics, telemetry or crash-reporting SDK (see Advertising below for what Google's ad SDK collects).

The only permission the app ever asks for is Apple's tracking question on iPhone (see Tracking below). It sends no notifications and has no chat, sharing or in-app purchase.

If you email us

If you write to us for support, we receive your email address and what you write. We use them only to reply to you. We don't add you to any mailing list and we don't share your message with anyone.

Advertising

Perennial is free and supported by one small banner ad at the bottom of the screen, served by Google AdMob. There are no full-screen, pop-up, video or rewarded ads.

To serve ads, Google's Mobile Ads SDK collects and processes certain information. According to Google's own data disclosure for the iOS SDK, it may collect the following (for Android, see Android below):

  • crash logs and other diagnostic information, used to diagnose problems and improve the SDK, and possibly also for advertising and analytics
  • performance data, such as app launch time, hang rate or energy usage
  • a device ID: the device's advertising identifier or another identifier limited to the app or developer (see Tracking below for when it is the advertising identifier)
  • advertising data, such as the ads you have seen
  • coarse location, derived from your IP address
  • product interactions, such as app launch taps, and interaction information, such as ad views

This is collected by Google, not by us. We never see it: AdMob gives us only aggregate reports, such as how many ads were shown.

Google's data disclosure for its iOS Mobile Ads SDK: developers.google.com/admob/ios/privacy/data-disclosure
How Google uses data from apps that use its services: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites
Google's privacy policy: policies.google.com/privacy

How the ads are set up

  • Personalized only if you allow it. Every banner request asks for non-personalized ads until the choice that applies to you says otherwise: on iPhone, until you allow tracking in Apple's question; in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, until you consent to personalized ads in Google's form; on Android elsewhere, personalized ads can be shown. Google says non-personalized ads "are not based on a user's past behavior" and use context instead, such as a coarse, city-level location, and that they "still use cookies or mobile ad identifiers for frequency capping and aggregated ad reporting".
  • Rated G. The maximum ad content rating is G, for general audiences.
  • For people 13 and older. The ads are not treated as child-directed.

Google's description: developers.google.com/admob/ios/targeting

Tracking (iPhone)

Perennial asks Apple's question once, after you continue past a short note. It never asks when you open the app or on the day you start. Once you have used it on a few days, it shows a short note, "A note from Perennial", with one button, Continue. Continue shows Apple's "Allow Perennial to track your activity across other companies' apps and websites?" question. Either answer is fine, and your habits stay the same.

  • Allow: the banner can use your device's advertising identifier (IDFA) to show ads that match your interests.
  • Ask App Not to Track: the banner stays non-personalized and gets an advertising identifier of all zeros.
  • You can change your answer any time: in the app, Settings, Personalized ads, or in iOS Settings, Privacy & Security, Tracking. If "Allow Apps to Request to Track" is off there, iOS never shows the question and the answer counts as no.
  • The app records only that it has asked, on your device. We don't see your answer.
  • We don't combine anything from the app with other companies' data, and we share nothing with data brokers. Google, as the ad provider, may use the identifier as it describes in its own policy.

The EEA, the UK and Switzerland

On your first launch Google's consent form appears over the loading screen, before any ad is requested. You can allow or refuse personalized ads and manage the details. If you refuse, the banner still shows, with non-personalized ads, and the app never shows the tracking note above. Settings, Privacy choices opens Google's privacy options so you can change your mind at any time. If the form can't be shown (for example, with no connection on a first launch), the app shows no ads that session and asks again on a later launch.

Google's description: support.google.com/admob/answer/10105530

US states with privacy laws

Where a US state privacy law applies, Google may offer its own opt-out; the app then shows the same Settings, Privacy choices row, which opens Google's options.

Android

Perennial on Android works the same way: the same habits kept only on your phone, and the same one banner.

  • What Google's ad SDK collects on Android. According to Google's data disclosure for its Android Mobile Ads SDK, it collects and shares, for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention: your device's IP address, which may be used to estimate its general location; product interactions, such as app launches and taps; diagnostic information, such as app launch time, hang rate and energy usage; and device identifiers. Google says all of it is encrypted in transit. This is collected by Google, not by us.
  • The advertising ID. Outside the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, the app uses Android's advertising ID (the com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID permission) so the banner can match your interests. You can reset it or opt out of ad personalization in Android Settings, under Privacy, Ads, which Settings, Personalized ads in the app opens for you. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form decides.
  • Permissions it doesn't have. The app removes Android's ad-services permissions (ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION and ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS), and it has no location, camera, microphone, contacts or notification permission.
  • The permissions it has are install-time permissions that never show a prompt: internet and network state (for the banner) and the advertising ID, plus any that Google's and Android's own libraries need to run briefly in the background.
  • Backups. Android may include Perennial's habits in your phone's own backup to your Google account and restore them on a new phone. That backup is governed by Google's privacy policy, not this one.

Google's data disclosure for its Android Mobile Ads SDK: developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/play-data-disclosure

Children

Perennial is for people 13 and older. The app has no accounts, chat, sharing or sign-up, it never asks anyone for personal information, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you think a child has emailed us personal information, write to us and we will delete it.

Your rights

  • Access - everything the app stores (your habits) is visible in the app.
  • Deletion - delete a habit in the app, or delete the app to remove all of its data from your device.
  • Emails - if you have emailed us, ask and we will delete your messages.

Apart from emails you choose to send, there is nothing for us to hold or delete. For data held by Google, use Google's own controls, described in its policies linked above.

Changes to this policy
