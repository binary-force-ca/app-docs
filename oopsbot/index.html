Oops Bot - Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 6, 2026

Oops Bot: Tic Tac Toe is a game of tic-tac-toe against Oops, a small cartoon robot. The app itself collects nothing about you and sends nothing to us. It has no account and no server, and the game works offline. The only third party involved is Google, which serves the one banner ad at the bottom of the screen. Every ad request is tagged as child-directed, rated G and non-personalized, and the app does not track you.

Information the app stores

Oops Bot keeps two small records, only on your own device:

  • your score: your wins, Oops's wins, draws, your current streak and your best streak
  • your settings: whether you play as X or O, who starts, whether "Oops tries harder" is on, whether Vibration is on, and (for "Take turns") who started the last game

A game in progress is not saved.

  • There is no account, no sign-up and no login.
  • There is no Oops Bot server. We do not operate a backend, and nothing you do in the game is uploaded to us.
  • We cannot see your score or your settings. Nobody can retrieve them from us, because we never receive them.
  • The game works fully offline. Only the banner ad needs a connection.

Because this data lives only on your device, deleting the app deletes it. It is not backed up to us and cannot be restored by us. If your device backs itself up (to iCloud on an iPhone, or with Android's backup to your Google account), Oops Bot's data may be included in that backup, which is governed by Apple's or Google's privacy policy, not this one.

Information the app does not collect

The app does not collect, transmit or have access to:

  • your name, email address, phone number or postal address
  • your precise location. The app never asks for location access. (Google may estimate a coarse location from your IP address to serve an ad; see Advertising below.)
  • your contacts, photos, camera, microphone or health data
  • usage analytics or crash reports of our own. The app contains no analytics, telemetry or crash-reporting SDK (see Advertising below for what Google's ad SDK collects).

The app never asks you for a permission: no location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos, notifications or tracking. Vibration uses your phone's built-in haptics, which need no prompt (on Android it is an install-time permission; see Android below). There is no chat, no sharing, no in-app purchase and no sound.

If you email us

The About section of the app's Settings has a Report an ad row. It opens your own mail app with a draft addressed to us. The draft names the app, its version and your iOS or Android version, and nothing else. You can edit it before sending, or not send it at all.

If you do email us, to report an ad or for support, we receive your email address and what you write. We use them only to reply to you and to look into the ad you reported (for example, to block it in our AdMob account). We don't add you to any mailing list and we don't share your message with anyone.

Advertising

Oops Bot is free and supported by one banner ad at the bottom of the screen, served by Google AdMob. There are never full-screen, pop-up, video or rewarded ads.

To serve ads, Google's Mobile Ads SDK collects and processes certain information. According to Google's own data disclosure for the iOS SDK, it may collect the following (for Android, see Android below):

  • crash logs, which Google describes as not related to the user, and other diagnostic information, used to diagnose problems and improve the SDK, and possibly also for advertising and analytics
  • performance data, such as app launch time, hang rate or energy usage, which Google describes as user-associated
  • a device ID: the device's advertising identifier or another identifier limited to the app or developer (see Tracking below for why it is not the advertising identifier in Oops Bot)
  • advertising data, such as the ads you have seen
  • coarse location, derived from your IP address (the app never asks for location access, so Google gets no location from your device's Location Services)
  • product interactions, such as app launch taps, and interaction information, such as video views

This is collected by Google, not by us. We never see it and never receive it: AdMob gives us only aggregate reports, such as how many ads were shown.

Google's data disclosure for its iOS Mobile Ads SDK: developers.google.com/admob/ios/privacy/data-disclosure
How Google uses data from apps that use its services: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites
Google's privacy policy: policies.google.com/privacy

How the ads are set up

  • Child-directed. Every ad request is tagged for child-directed treatment under the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA). Google says that for such requests it "takes steps to disable IBA and remarketing ads", meaning interest-based ads and ads that follow you from other apps and sites, and that the tag "prevents the transmission of the Advertising Identifier, IDFA."
  • Non-personalized. Every banner request asks for non-personalized ads. Google says these "are not based on a user's past behavior" and use context instead, such as a coarse, city-level location. Google also says they "still use cookies or mobile ad identifiers for frequency capping and aggregated ad reporting": limiting how often you see the same ad, and counting how often ads are shown and tapped.
  • Rated G. The maximum ad content rating is G, for general audiences.

What these settings don't change: Google's SDK still collects the kinds of data listed under Advertising, to deliver the ad, measure it and keep it working. Google's disclosure does not describe less collection for child-directed requests, so we don't claim it.

Google's description of these settings: developers.google.com/admob/ios/targeting and support.google.com/admob/answer/7676680

Tracking (iOS)

Oops Bot does not track you, so it never shows Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt.

  • The app never asks for permission to track. Apple's documentation says that on iOS 14.5 and later, an app that hasn't requested tracking authorization gets an advertising identifier (IDFA) of all zeros.
  • The child-directed tag tells Google's SDK not to send the IDFA, and turns off interest-based ads and remarketing.
  • Ads are chosen from the context, not from a profile of you. We don't combine anything from the app with other companies' data, and we share nothing with data brokers.

The EEA, the UK and Switzerland

The app shows no consent form. For ad requests from the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland that carry no consent signal from a Google-certified consent platform, Google serves only "Limited Ads". Google says Limited Ads "disable the collection, sharing, and use of personal data for personalization of ads" and "disable all personalization and features that require use of a local identifier". It adds: "Data such as IP addresses will still be used to facilitate the transmission of the ad to the device." In these regions you may see fewer ads, or none. The game works the same either way.

Google's description: support.google.com/admob/answer/10105530

Android

Oops Bot on Android works the same way: the same game, the same score and settings kept only on your phone, the same one banner, and every ad request child-directed, rated G and non-personalized.

  • What Google's ad SDK collects on Android. According to Google's data disclosure for its Android Mobile Ads SDK, it collects and shares, for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention: your device's IP address, which may be used to estimate its general location; product interactions, such as app launches and taps; diagnostic information, such as app launch time, hang rate and energy usage; and device identifiers. Google says all of it is encrypted in transit. This is collected by Google, not by us.
  • No advertising ID. The child-directed tag tells Google's SDK not to send the Android advertising ID, and the app also removes the advertising-ID permission (com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID) from its manifest, so Android gives it an advertising ID of all zeros. Google's SDK can still use identifiers that aren't the advertising ID, such as the app set ID and a rotating SDK instance ID, which Google lists under device identifiers.
  • Permissions it doesn't have. The app also removes Android's ad-services permissions (ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION and ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS), so there are no Privacy Sandbox interest topics or ad attribution, and it has no storage, overlay, location, camera, microphone, contacts or notification permission.
  • The permissions it has are install-time permissions that never show a prompt: internet and network state (for the banner), vibration (for the haptics), and two that Google's and Android's own libraries need to run in the background briefly (wake lock and a foreground service).
  • Backups. Android may include Oops Bot's score and settings in your phone's own backup to your Google account, and restore them when you set up a new phone. That backup is governed by Google's privacy policy, not this one. We never receive it.
  • Report an ad opens your mail app with a draft that names your Android version, as described under "If you email us".

Google's data disclosure for its Android Mobile Ads SDK: developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/play-data-disclosure
Google's description of the child-directed setting on Android: developers.google.com/admob/android/targeting

Children

Oops Bot is a general-audience game, and families often play it together. Because a younger player may be holding the phone, every ad request is treated as child-directed (see "How the ads are set up"). The app has no accounts, chat, sharing or sign-up, it never asks anyone for personal information, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you think a child has emailed us personal information, write to us and we will delete it.

Your rights

  • Access - everything the app stores (your score and your settings) is visible in the app.
  • Deletion - delete the app to remove all of its data from your device. This version has no in-app reset.
  • Emails - if you have emailed us, ask and we will delete your messages.

Apart from emails you choose to send, there is nothing for us to hold or delete. For data held by Google, use Google's own controls, described in its policies linked above.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the "Last updated" date above will change. Material changes will be reflected in an app update.
