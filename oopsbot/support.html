Frequently asked questions

How do I play?

Tap any empty square to place your mark. Oops thinks for a moment, then places its own. Three of the same mark in a line, across, down or diagonally, wins. If the board fills up with no line, it's a draw. Tap New game to play again. Your score is under Oops's speech bubble.

Why is Oops so easy to beat?

That's the idea. Oops picks a random empty square, on purpose, and doesn't pretend otherwise. It isn't broken: Oops Bot is meant to be a relaxed game you can usually win, with a robot that is a good sport about it. Oops still wins now and then, when its guesses happen to line up.

What does "Oops tries harder" do?

Open Settings with the gear in the top-right corner and turn on Oops tries harder. Oops then grabs a win when it sees one and sometimes blocks yours. The rest of the time it's still guessing, so you can still beat it. Look for the sweatband on its forehead: Oops wears it in every game where it's trying harder. Changes start on a fresh board, never in the middle of a game.

Can I play as O, or let Oops start?

Yes. In Settings, Play as picks X or O, and Who starts picks You, Oops, or Take turns. Your marks are always indigo and Oops's are coral, whichever letter you play, and X and O also differ by shape.

What does Vibration do?

With Vibration on in Settings, you feel a light tap when you place a mark and a buzz when a game ends. Turn it off and the app stops using haptics completely. If it's on and you feel nothing, check that haptics are turned on in your iPhone's Sounds & Haptics settings, or on Android in Settings › Sound & vibration › Vibration & haptics.

How do I reset my score?

This version has no reset button. To start from zero, delete the app and install it again. That clears your score and your streaks, and also resets your settings.

Is my score backed up?

Not by us. Your score and settings are stored only on your phone. There is no account and no server, which means we cannot see them, and equally cannot recover them for you. Deleting the app deletes them. If your iPhone backs itself up to iCloud, or your Android phone backs up to your Google account, Oops Bot's data may be included in that backup, and a new phone set up from it may bring your score back.

Why is there an ad? Can I remove it?

Oops Bot is free and supported by one small banner at the bottom of the screen. There are never full-screen, pop-up or video ads. There is no way to remove the banner in this version. Every ad request is child-directed, rated G and non-personalized; the privacy policy explains what that means.

I saw an ad that seems wrong. How do I report it?

Open Settings (the gear), scroll to About and tap Report an ad. Your mail app opens with a message to us. Tell us what the ad showed and when you saw it; a screenshot helps. If no mail app opens, write to julia.gatina.ca@gmail.com directly.

Does it work offline?

Yes, completely. Only the banner needs a connection. Without one, the game plays exactly the same and the space at the bottom stays empty.

Does Oops Bot track me?

No. The app has no account and no analytics of its own, never asks for permission to track you, and never shows a tracking prompt. See the privacy policy for what Google's ad SDK collects.

Does it work with VoiceOver?

Yes. Every square is labeled, for example "Row 1, column 2, empty", and Oops's moves and the result of each game are announced. When Reduce Motion is on, Oops's animations are turned off.

Does it work on Android?

Yes, on Android phones, with the same game, settings and score. A few things work the Android way:

  • The Back button or gesture closes Settings. On the game it sends the app to the background, and your game is still there when you come back.
  • TalkBack reads the same labels as VoiceOver, and Oops's moves and results are announced. When Remove animations is on (Settings › Accessibility), Oops's animations are turned off.
  • Changing the font size, display size, bold text or language while a game is in progress restarts that game. Your score and settings are kept.
  • The app never asks for a permission, and it has no access to Android's advertising ID.
